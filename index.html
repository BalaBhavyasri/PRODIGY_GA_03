Markov Core

What is a Markov Chain?

Click to Flip

Statistical Dependency

A Markov Chain is a model where the probability of the next event (character/word) depends only on the current state prefix, disregarding all older history.

P(X_t | X_{t-1}, ..., X_1) = P(X_t | X_{t-1})

Chain History Size

The "Order" defines the prefix length of "previous ones" used to look up probabilities.
• 1st Order: predicts based on 1 previous item.

Tokens & Vocab

• Word-based: Computes word probabilities. Sentences are more coherent but need a large training set.
• Char-based: Computes character letter probabilities. Learns spelling and invents words, but takes longer to form valid syntax.

Probability Math

How are rules computed?

Click to Flip

Relative Frequencies

The probability of word \(W_t\) following prefix \(P\) is:
\[P(W_t | P) = \frac{\text{Count}(P + W_t)}{\text{Total Count}(P)}\] If "the theory" is followed by "of" 8 times out of 10, the probability is 80%.

Count(P + Next) / Count(P)

Temperature

How do we scale random choice?

Click to Flip

Sampling Distribution

Adjusts probabilities using: \(w_i = c_i^{1/T}\).
• Low T (< 0.5): Highly deterministic (only highest probability choice).
• High T (> 1.2): Highly random (flattens probability distribution).

Softmax exponent = 1 / T
